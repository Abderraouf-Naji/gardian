\subsection{Where the gain comes from (RQ2)}
\label{sec:ablation}

RQ2 asks three things at once: whether the retrieval gain is in the
\emph{learned branch scores}, whether the \emph{per-query} controller adds
anything on top of one global weight on those same scores, and whether the
gap to a perfect mix is still a modelling problem or is already limited by
the labels. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports nDCG@10 as mean$\pm$std over the
five seeds $\{13,21,42,87,100\}$, on all four hybrid families. Every row
reuses the identical branch outputs from one forward pass. \textbf{NoSparse}
and \textbf{NoDense} keep only the dense or sparse branch.
\textbf{Fixed-$\alpha$} mixes both branches with a single $\alpha$ fitted on
the development split (PubMedQA-Labeled borrows PubMedQA-Artificial
development) and applied unchanged at test. \textbf{GARDIAN} replaces that
constant with the controller. \textbf{Oracle-$\alpha$} picks, per query, the
$\alpha$ that maximises nDCG@10 on the \emph{test} labels; it is a ceiling on
this fusion family, not a system.

A uniform $0.5$/$0.5$ mix is \emph{not} the control. On
SPLADE\texttt{++}+MedCPT / MedMCQA it scores $45.4$, against $47.9$ for
Fixed-$\alpha$ and $48.0$ for GARDIAN: beating $0.5$ mostly shows that $0.5$
is a bad constant. Question-type conditioning is not a model input and is
not ablated.

\paragraph{Against single-channel branches.}
The learned mix is worth a channel, not a rounding error. Relative to the
better of NoSparse and NoDense, Fixed-$\alpha$ gains $+1.0$ to $+2.0$ nDCG@10
on PQA-L, $+4.7$ to $+6.7$ on PQA-A, and $+6.0$ to $+7.1$ on the two BM25
hybrids on MedMCQA. The dropped channel is dataset-dependent: NoDense (sparse
only) is the weak row on both PubMedQA splits, while NoSparse (dense only)
collapses MedMCQA to $32.6$--$32.8$ on every family. With SPLADE\texttt{++} on
MedMCQA the sparse branch already carries most of the work (NoDense
$46.8$/$47.1$); mixing the dense branch still adds about one point. Recall@20
moves with nDCG@10. The same MedMCQA NoSparse drop is $71.9\to 60.5$ on
BM25+FAISS.

\paragraph{Against Fixed-$\alpha$.}
Per-query weighting is not where the gain is. GARDIAN minus Fixed-$\alpha$ is
$+0.11$, $+0.18$ and $+0.28$ on BM25+FAISS, and at most $+0.44$ anywhere
(PQA-A, SPLADE\texttt{++}+MedCPT). On PQA-L with a MedCPT dense channel the
controller \emph{loses}: $-0.45$ on BM25+MedCPT and $-0.66$ on
SPLADE\texttt{++}+MedCPT, where Fixed-$\alpha$ is the best system in the
table. On SPLADE\texttt{++}+FAISS / MedMCQA the two rows are tied to $0.1$
points and Fixed-$\alpha$ is slightly ahead. A reviewer who is shown only a
$0.5$/$0.5$ ``NoCtrl'' row would read a $+1$ to $+2.5$ controller effect that
the honest control does not support.

\paragraph{Against the Oracle-$\alpha$ ceiling.}
The remaining headroom is real, but it is not a better controller waiting to
be trained. Oracle-$\alpha$ sits $+3.0$ to $+3.2$ above GARDIAN on PQA-L,
$+5.0$ to $+5.3$ on PQA-A, and $+7.8$ to $+9.0$ on MedMCQA. On PQA-L every
query has gold in the pool, so that $+3$ is ranking headroom; at the same
time $28$--$37\%$ of PQA-L queries are invariant to $\alpha$ and more than
half of the $\alpha$ grid ties the per-query maximum, so much of the oracle
number is post-hoc selection among ties. On MedMCQA $12$--$16\%$ of queries
have no gold in the pool ($825$ of $5094$ on BM25+FAISS) and score $0$ for every
row, including the oracle; pool recall is $0.84$--$0.88$. Another
$\approx 29\%$ of MedMCQA queries are $\alpha$-invariant. Incomplete labels
and a flat mixing surface, not a missing architecture, are what keep the
ceiling from being a target.

The honest summary is therefore: GARDIAN's retrieval improvement is learned
branch scoring over complementary channels. Query-adaptive fusion is a small
and sometimes negative increment on a well-tuned global $\alpha$ over those
branches. The oracle gap should be read as a bound on this fusion family
under incomplete and often $\alpha$-insensitive labels, not as evidence that
a better per-query mixer is still available.
