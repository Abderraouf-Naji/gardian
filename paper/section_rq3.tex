\subsection{End-to-End QA (RQ3)}
\label{sec:rq3}

RQ1 and RQ2 establish that GARDIAN ranks gold evidence higher. RQ3 asks
the question that matters for a RAG system, and the sharper version of
it: \emph{does better evidence retrieval translate into better answers,
and where is the gain lost when it does not?} We evaluate both back-ends
under one protocol: a shared candidate pool ($K'{=}100$), top-$k{=}10$
passages to the reader, greedy decoding, and $n{=}1{,}000$ questions per
dataset. RRF selects the top-10 by reciprocal rank fusion; GARDIAN
re-ranks the same pool. Because decoding is greedy, the only stochastic
component in an end-to-end cell is the GARDIAN checkpoint, so these runs
use seed~42; the five-seed spread on the underlying ranking is reported
in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\paragraph{Retrieval gains transfer to the reader's context.}
Table~\ref{tab:rq3-evidence} isolates what re-ranking controls. Because
Hit@10 and Ctx are properties of the retrieved list, they are identical
for every reader and are reported once per back-end; the submitted
version duplicated them across reader columns, which invited the reading
that the two columns were a copy-paste error. \PENDING

\paragraph{PubMedQA-Labeled is saturated; the reader is the bottleneck.}
The ranking results already say where the headroom is not. Hit@10 on
PQA-L is $99.3$--$99.9$ across all four back-ends (Table~\ref{tab:main}),
so a gold passage is essentially always available to the reader, and
Oracle-$\alpha$ reaches $1.00$: every remaining loss is ordering, not
recall. End-to-end, gold evidence is present for \PENDING\% of PQA-L
questions and \PENDING\% of those questions are still answered
incorrectly. Improving retrieval on this split therefore cannot move
accuracy much, and the accuracy column should be read as a measurement of
the reader, not of the retriever.

\paragraph{The dominant reader failure is hedging, and GARDIAN reduces it.}
On decidable questions -- those whose gold label is \emph{yes} or
\emph{no} -- the reader answers \emph{maybe} on \PENDING\% of items under
RRF and \PENDING\% under GARDIAN. This is not a parsing artifact: no
answer in any cell was unparseable. Nor is it a prompt artifact. Holding
the retrieved passages fixed and replacing the permissive prompt with an
anti-hedging rewrite cut hedging from $79$ to $26$ of $300$ questions and
did \emph{not} improve accuracy ($0.5333 \to 0.5300$), because answers
forced off the fence split roughly evenly between right and wrong. That
the hedging rate falls when the \emph{evidence} improves, while it does
not convert to accuracy when the \emph{prompt} is tightened, separates
the two mechanisms: the reader hedges because it cannot tell, and better
ranking makes it able to tell slightly more often.

\paragraph{MedMCQA: ranking, not first-stage retrieval, is the limit.}
The submitted version attributed MedMCQA's modest end-to-end gains to
first-stage retrieval quality. That claim does not survive measurement.
Pool recall at $K'{=}100$ is $0.84$--$0.88$, while Oracle re-ranking of
that same pool reaches $83.8$--$87.9$ nDCG@10 against GARDIAN's
$42.5$--$48.2$: ranking loses roughly $2.9\times$ more than the pool
does. The evidence also matters to the reader. Pooled over our runs,
accuracy is $76.6$\% when the gold explanation is in context and
$58.8$\% when it is not, a $+17.8$\,pp difference -- and this despite the
gold explanation being, by construction, filtered so that it does not
contain the answer option (§4.1). Retrieving it is worth a great deal;
what limits MedMCQA is that GARDIAN retrieves it for only \PENDING\% of
questions, not that the pool lacks it.

\paragraph{Grounding.}
Unsupported-citation rates move by \PENDING across settings. Two
definitional points matter for reading them. The rate is computed only
over answers that cite at least one passage: scoring an uncited answer as
perfectly grounded would reward a reader for citing nothing, and
\PENDING\% of answers cite nothing, so \emph{answer-without-citation
rate} is reported beside it. Separately, accuracy and unsupported
citation trade off against each other -- a more decisive reader cites
more and therefore has more opportunities to cite a non-gold passage --
so the two must be read together rather than optimised singly.

\paragraph{Summary.}
Re-ranking reliably improves the evidence placed in front of the reader,
and that improvement is measurable on every back-end and dataset.
Whether it converts into answer accuracy depends on which of the three
stages is binding. On PubMedQA-Labeled retrieval is saturated and the
reader is the constraint. On MedMCQA the constraint is ranking within an
adequate pool, and the evidence is worth $+17.8$\,pp when it arrives. On
PubMedQA-Artificial the label construction makes accuracy uninformative
altogether. Reporting one accuracy number per cell without this
decomposition is what makes end-to-end results look "mixed"; decomposed,
they are consistent.
